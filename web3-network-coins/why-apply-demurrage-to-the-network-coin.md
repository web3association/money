# Why apply demurrage to the network coin?

Demurrage is a recurring charge applied to the possession of a monetary balance. Its purpose is to increase the cost of prolonged retention and create an incentive to spend, lend, invest or otherwise make the asset available.

A network coin introduces additional considerations because it is not merely a transferable monetary asset. It may also provide access to network resources, compensate node operators, support consensus, influence governance and provide liquidity or collateral.

Applying demurrage to the network coin is intended to make an asset required by the entire network more available, productive and difficult to concentrate indefinitely. When the charge is transferred to a treasury, it can also provide recurring income for node operation, development and other useful work.

These outcomes depend on the rate, implementation and use of the collected coins. Demurrage does not automatically create productive investment, decentralised ownership or effective treasury expenditure. It creates incentives and resources that can make those outcomes more achievable.



## **Digital money is highly storable**

A digital network coin does not physically deteriorate. It does not rot, rust or require physical storage space, and a large balance can be held using the same type of wallet as a small balance.

The holder may face market risk, custody risk and the possibility of losing access to their credentials, but the monetary units themselves do not deteriorate. Once a participant has secured control of the relevant credentials, the direct cost of leaving the balance idle may be very low.

Staking and financial protocols can make retention even more attractive. Instead of merely preserving the balance, a holder may receive additional coins while maintaining exposure to the asset’s price.

If the network grows and the coin appreciates, the holder can benefit from both increasing coin ownership and increasing purchasing power. This creates strong incentives to accumulate and retain the asset.

Demurrage changes the incentive attached to passive ownership by imposing a continuing cost on prolonged retention.



## **The network coin is a shared operational resource**

The network coin is privately controlled by individual holders, but its availability affects shared infrastructure.

Users and transaction sponsors may need it to pay for network resources. Node operators receive it as compensation, and it may also be required for staking, governance, liquidity and collateral.

Everyone using the network therefore depends on the coin remaining obtainable. If a large proportion of the supply is held idle, the nominal supply still exists, but fewer units are available through markets or productive positions.

A concentrated and inactive supply can make the cost of obtaining the coin more volatile. It may also give large holders increasing influence over consensus and governance.

Demurrage applies a carrying cost to private control of this operational resource. Holders retain ownership and the freedom to decide how to use their coins, but indefinite inactivity is no longer costless.



## **Increasing coin availability**

A large nominal supply does not guarantee practical availability. Coins may remain concentrated in inactive wallets or positions that do not make them accessible to other users.

When retention has little cost, holders can wait indefinitely for higher prices and remove coins from the market. A user may need only a small amount for a transaction, but that amount must still be obtainable from someone willing to sell, lend or provide it.

Demurrage changes this calculation. A holder leaving coins idle experiences a gradual reduction in their balance, making it more attractive to sell, lend, spend or invest them.

This can increase the amount available through token exchanges and lending protocols. Users and applications can obtain the coin more easily, reducing the risk that a small number of holders control access to an asset required by the wider ecosystem.

Greater availability does not require every coin to change hands constantly. Coins committed to staking, liquidity, lending or collateral may remain in a position for an extended period while still supporting network operation or making resources available to others.



## **Increasing coin velocity**

Coin velocity describes how frequently the network coin changes hands during a given period.

If a large proportion of the supply remains in the same wallets for years, the actively circulating supply may be much smaller than the nominal supply. Demurrage creates an incentive to move idle coins into active economic use.

A holder may spend the coin on goods or services, use it for network fees, provide liquidity, lend it or exchange it for another asset. The same units can then support multiple transactions over time.

Higher and more consistent velocity allows a given nominal supply to support more network activity. The protocol may have less need to create new coins merely because existing units have been withdrawn from circulation.

The objective is not to maximise velocity without limit. Users require working balances, node operators need reserves, and staking, collateral and long-term liquidity positions may provide value while remaining committed.

The objective is greater reliability. Demurrage can make the actively available supply less dependent on changing confidence and speculative expectations.



## **Encouraging productive use through granular incentives**

Demurrage changes the relative cost of leaving coins inactive while allowing the network to create precise incentives for particular uses.

An inactive wallet balance can pay the base rate, while recognised staking, token-exchange liquidity or other productive use cases could receive different reductions according to the value they provide.

This is more granular than general inflation or uniform rewards, which will affect coins regardless of how they are used.

It also encourages productive activity without requiring the network to create large additional rewards, because holders benefit by avoiding part of the standard carrying charge.

These reductions should be limited to clearly valuable, durable and verifiable positions, as overly broad or activity-based incentives could encourage artificial transactions or other forms of avoidance.



## **Reducing concentration and supporting dispersion**

Low-cost storage combined with positive returns can cause ownership to become more concentrated over time.

A participant with a sufficiently large balance may fund their expenses using only part of their staking or lending return and reinvest the remainder. Their ownership can continue increasing without a corresponding new contribution of labour, goods or services.

A proportional carrying charge makes large balances more costly to maintain in nominal terms. If the effective demurrage rate exceeds the low-risk return available from staking or lending, existing ownership cannot grow indefinitely through passive or low-risk activity alone.

Holders can still increase their balances by providing goods, services, labour, capital or risk-bearing investment. Demurrage does not prevent wealth accumulation. It reduces the advantage attached to ownership itself by requiring continuing income or contribution to preserve a large position.

Demurrage can also support coin dispersion. Holders have an incentive to transfer coins in exchange for value rather than allow their balances to decline. Coins may move towards workers, businesses, developers and service providers or enter exchange and lending markets where other users can obtain them.

Treasury expenditure can reinforce this effect by distributing collected coins to node operators, developers and other contributors.

The outcome depends on how the income is used. Returning collected coins proportionately to existing holders would weaken the effect, while repeatedly funding the same organisations could reinforce concentration.



## **Improving consensus decentralisation**

In a proof-of-stake network, coin ownership can translate into influence over consensus. A concentrated supply may allow a small group to control a substantial proportion of validation power, coordinate censorship or otherwise influence the accepted ledger state.

Demurrage makes a large position more difficult to preserve indefinitely without continued contribution or expenditure. Treasury payments can also distribute coins to a wider population of operators and contributors, allowing more participants to stake and validate transactions.

The effect depends on the treatment of staking. Staked coins may receive a reduced rate because they contribute to network security, but the reduction should not create a low-risk net return that allows large holders to increase their ownership continuously.

An excessive rate could also weaken security. If demurrage causes demand and market value to decline substantially, an attacker may be able to acquire a significant position more cheaply.

Demurrage supports consensus decentralisation only when the rate discourages persistent concentration without undermining demand for the coin.



## **Improving governance decentralisation**

If coin ownership affects voting power, the distribution of the coin also affects governance.

Large holdings can otherwise remain intact indefinitely and grow through staking or financial yields. The resulting influence over protocol rules, treasury expenditure and monetary policy may become permanent without requiring continued contribution.

Demurrage makes ownership-based influence costly to preserve. This can make governance more responsive to continuing participation rather than historical ownership alone.

It does not remove every governance problem. Wealthy participants can purchase additional coins, coordinate through organisations or obtain substantial treasury funding. Governance must also address delegation, participation, conflicts of interest and treasury capture.

Demurrage provides one supporting mechanism for resisting the persistence and compounding of ownership-based power.



## **Creating predictable network income**

A periodic charge on network-coin balances can provide more predictable nominal income than transaction fees alone.

Transaction-fee income changes with the number and type of transactions submitted. Activity may rise during periods of high demand and decline during economic contractions or shifts to competing networks.

Demurrage income depends primarily on the coin supply and the rates applying to different balances and positions. The network can estimate the expected income over longer periods and plan expenditure with greater certainty.

Transferring them to a treasury keeps the units within the economy and provides an ongoing source of funding. The treasury must return the coins to circulation through node compensation and payments for useful work. Allowing them to accumulate indefinitely would reproduce the inactivity demurrage is intended to discourage.



## **Providing reliable node-operator compensation**

Demurrage income could subsidise or fund node operation.

Under a fee-only model, operator income declines when transaction volume falls even though the network must continue operating. The protocol must estimate future activity, while operators remain exposed to changes in transaction volume and the coin’s value.

A recurring balance charge provides a more stable nominal base from which operators can be compensated. More predictable funding can support a stable and decentralised operator population.

Operators remain exposed to the coin’s purchasing power. A predictable number of coins does not guarantee that the income will cover real operating expenses if the market value declines.

Balanced demand and moderate price stability therefore remain important under a demurrage-funded model.



## **Reducing transaction fees**

If demurrage income pays part of the cost of node operation, the network can reduce its dependence on transaction fees.

This spreads part of the operating cost across coin holders rather than placing the full burden on active users. Holders benefit from a maintained and secure network, as well as any appreciation associated with network growth, even when they do not transact.

Lower fees reduce barriers to participation and allow low-value exchanges that would be uneconomic under higher fees. This can be especially important for inexpensive purchases and users in lower-income economies.

Some transaction charge may still be required to allocate scarce network resources and prevent spam. The advantage is that fees can focus on resource management rather than providing the network’s entire operating budget.

A balance charge does not align costs and benefits perfectly. Small holders may depend more heavily on liquid balances, while wealthy participants may have greater access to positions receiving reduced rates. The implementation can try to account for these differences.



## **Aligning network funding incentives**

Under a transaction fee based model, active users bear the cost of maintaining the network. People who hold the coin without transacting make no direct contribution, even though they benefit from the network’s security, maintenance and growth.

Demurrage spreads part of this cost across holders according to the amount of the network coin they control and the applicable rate.

Participants who make their coins available through recognised productive positions can receive reductions, while inactive holders bear more of the cost. This connects the contribution more closely to both the benefits of holding the coin and the way it is used.

The alignment is not perfect. Small holders may depend more heavily on liquid balances, while wealthier participants may have greater access to positions receiving reduced rates. The implementation must account for these differences.



## **Funding maintenance and development**

A genesis allocation can fund development during the network’s early stages, but it is finite. A recurring balance charge can provide long-term treasury income after that allocation has been spent.

The income can support protocol maintenance, security reviews, research, technical improvements and application infrastructure. Predictable funding also allows contributors to plan work extending across several years.

This can reduce dependence on donations, founding organisations and private investors. Private investment remains useful for commercial applications, but it may not fund open-source infrastructure or shared standards whose benefits cannot be captured by a single investor.

Treasury funding can support this work without requiring every project to create a separate token or rent-generating mechanism.

The effectiveness of the model depends on governance. Collecting income does not guarantee productive allocation. A poorly governed treasury can waste resources, repeatedly fund the same organisations or become a target for political and financial capture.



## **Creating an ecosystem growth cycle**

If treasury expenditure produces benefits greater than its cost, demurrage income can accelerate network development.

Improved infrastructure can attract users and developers. New applications create additional reasons to use the network and increase demand for the coin.

Greater demand can raise the coin’s market value, increasing the purchasing power of future demurrage income. This can create a reinforcing cycle in which funding improves the ecosystem, ecosystem growth increases coin demand and stronger demand increases the value of future funding.

The cycle is not guaranteed. Funded initiatives may fail to produce sufficient value, and a high carrying charge may discourage investment more than treasury expenditure encourages adoption.

The community must therefore evaluate the results of funded work rather than assume that greater expenditure always produces greater growth.



## **Capturing part of coin appreciation**

A fixed or slowly changing coin supply may appreciate as network activity grows.

Existing holders benefit from this appreciation even when they make no further contribution. The increase in demand may result from work performed by developers, node operators, businesses and users across the network.

A percentage-based charge allows the network to capture part of that growth and return it to the ecosystem. Holders may possess fewer units while their remaining coins increase in purchasing power.

During a growth phase, appreciation may exceed the carrying charge, allowing both treasury funding and a positive return for holders. Demurrage is therefore directed at passive accumulation of units rather than necessarily eliminating every investment return.

This can connect investment returns more closely to the success of the ecosystem rather than relying solely on engineered scarcity. As the network matures, productive participation will become more important than passive retention.



## **Supporting flexible supply policies**

Demurrage does not require a fixed, expansionary, contractionary or elastic coin supply.

Collected coins can enter the treasury while the total supply remains fixed, be removed under a contractionary policy or operate alongside the creation of new units.

This provides two separate tools. Supply policy determines how many units exist, while demurrage influences the cost of retaining them.

More consistent circulation may reduce the scale of supply adjustments required to support network activity. Without demurrage, a decline in velocity may create pressure to issue more coins even though the existing supply is theoretically sufficient. Previously inactive balances may later return to circulation alongside the newly created units.

By encouraging existing coins to remain available, demurrage can make smaller supply changes more effective and predictable.

It does not eliminate the need to consider supply, and it cannot compensate for unlimited issuance or rapid changes in demand.



## **Funding wider public goods**

If the network achieves widespread adoption, even a low percentage charge could eventually generate income beyond the amount required for node operation, maintenance and ecosystem development.

Some of this income could support scientific, engineering, medical or environmental work considered beneficial to the wider global community.

For example, a 1 per cent annual charge on a network coin with a total market value of £10 billion represents approximately £100 million in nominal annual coin value. At a market value of £1 trillion, it represents approximately £10 billion.

These amounts do not guarantee equivalent spendable income because distributing or selling coins can affect their price. They illustrate the potential scale of a mature network treasury.

Public-goods funding would require legitimate governance and protection against capture. It is an opportunity created by demurrage income, not a requirement for operating the coin.



## **Demurrage does not remove every source of concentration**

Demurrage cannot ensure that ownership becomes broadly distributed.

Participants may continue accumulating coins through successful businesses, risky investments, control of productive resources or preferential financial opportunities.

Treasury expenditure and node compensation can also increase concentration if funding repeatedly flows to the same organisations. Node rewards may reinforce existing power if operation becomes dominated by a small number of providers.

Demurrage addresses one particular mechanism: the ability to preserve and increase a large monetary position through low-cost, low-risk retention.

Other forms of concentration require separate responses involving governance, competition, market structure and treasury design.



## **The central trade-off**

Applying demurrage creates a tension between circulation and demand.

A higher charge strengthens the incentive to avoid idle storage, increases potential treasury income and makes large balances harder to preserve. The same charge makes the coin less attractive to hold relative to tokens and external assets.

Users may sell the coin immediately after receiving it and acquire only what they need for fees. If this behaviour becomes widespread, the coin’s market value may decline, weakening node compensation, consensus security and treasury purchasing power.

A lower charge preserves demand but may be insufficient to discourage inactivity and concentration.

Demurrage must therefore operate alongside durable sources of demand, including network access, consensus participation, financial liquidity and collateral. Its rate and implementation must encourage availability, productive use and dispersion without making the coin so costly to hold that the network’s economic foundation is weakened.

The preceding analysis suggests an initial implementation based on a periodic network-coin charge, a simple supply policy and reduced carrying charges for selected productive uses.

The purpose of this design is to make prolonged idle storage costly while preserving sufficient demand for the coin to support node operation, network security and financial liquidity.

The implementation should begin simply. A new network will not have enough evidence to determine the ideal demurrage rate, supply policy or set of financial incentives in advance. Each additional mechanism creates governance and technical risks.

The initial system should therefore use a small number of predictable rules that can be evaluated and adjusted as the network develops.



## **A periodic network-coin charge**

The preferred implementation is a recurring percentage charge applied to network-coin balances.

Unlike coin expiry, a balance charge does not create several vintages of the same coin. Every unit remains fungible and usable regardless of when it was created or received.

Unlike a reward-only system, the charge directly changes the cost of inactivity. A holder cannot preserve a balance indefinitely without either paying the charge or making the coins available through a qualifying use.

The protocol would express the charge as an annual rate but collect or account for it in small amounts throughout the year.

For example, an annual rate of 3 per cent would not need to be removed in one large annual transaction. The corresponding portion could be applied daily or at another regular interval.

Frequent application creates a continuous incentive and avoids sudden market activity around a single collection date.



## **Applying the charge consistently**

The network-coin charge should apply to balances held by both user wallets and smart contracts.

If only ordinary wallets were charged, holders could place coins in a simple contract and avoid demurrage without providing any productive function. This would undermine the mechanism and favour participants with the technical ability to construct avoidance arrangements.

The protocol should determine the carrying cost according to the amount and duration of the balance rather than the type of address holding it.

Smart contracts should not receive a general exemption. Coins held in an inactive contract are economically similar to coins held in an inactive wallet.

Reduced rates can apply when a contract performs a recognised function, such as providing liquidity or securing an agreement. The reduction should result from that productive use rather than from the fact that the coins are held in a contract.

Founding entities, governments, applications and treasury participants should not receive arbitrary exemptions. Special treatment would weaken equal application and create incentives to route holdings through privileged organisations.



## **Calculating demurrage over time**

The economic cost should depend on how long a participant controls the balance.

Moving coins between addresses should not reset the holding period in a way that allows the charge to be avoided. Otherwise, participants could transfer coins between wallets they control before each collection event.

The protocol should account for demurrage according to elapsed time. It does not necessarily need to update every balance continuously. The accumulated charge can be calculated when a balance is accessed or when another relevant ledger event occurs, provided that the resulting amount corresponds to the published rate.

Users and applications should be able to calculate the expected cost independently. The formula should be public, predictable and simple enough for wallets and smart contracts to incorporate.

Long-term agreements must also be able to anticipate how the underlying balance will change. A contract holding one thousand coins for a year should be able to determine approximately how many will remain after demurrage.



## **Treasury collection rather than automatic burning**

The collected coins should initially be transferred to the network treasury rather than permanently removed from supply.

Treasury collection keeps the total coin supply unchanged. The charge changes ownership and creates circulation, but it does not automatically create a contractionary monetary policy.

Burning the coins would reduce the total supply. This could create appreciation that rewards continued holding and partially offsets the carrying cost.

A contractionary effect would also make the coin less available and increase the real burden of loans denominated in it. These outcomes conflict with the objective of using demurrage to improve circulation.

Treasury collection provides a separate benefit by creating predictable income for node operation, maintenance and ecosystem development.

The treasury must return the collected coins to circulation through expenditure. If it accumulates an increasing balance without spending it, the network merely transfers monetary inactivity from private holders to a collectively governed account.



## **Initial fixed supply**

A fixed coin supply is the simplest starting point.

Collected coins move to the treasury and are later distributed to node operators, contributors and other recipients. No new units are created, and none are removed.

A fixed supply allows participants to understand the coin’s basic monetary structure without relying on external data or discretionary issuance.

This is particularly important during the early development of the network. The long-term relationship between adoption, coin velocity, demurrage and purchasing power will not yet be known.

Introducing an elastic supply mechanism immediately would require assumptions about how demand should be measured and how the protocol should respond. These assumptions could be wrong or manipulated.

A fixed supply allows the network to gather evidence before adding complexity.



## **Responding to appreciation**

A fixed supply may cause the coin to appreciate as the network grows.

Some appreciation can support early investment and network security. However, rapid appreciation encourages storage, increases debt burdens and creates volatility in token-exchange markets.

The initial response should be to evaluate whether the demurrage rate offsets the net benefit from passive holding. The network does not need to react to every short-term price movement.

If appreciation remains persistently high and disrupts the coin’s use as liquidity or collateral, a modest expansionary policy can be considered.

Any expansion should begin at a low and predictable rate. New coins could be directed towards the treasury or node compensation.

An elastic supply should be considered only when reliable data and secure adjustment mechanisms exist. Perfect price stability does not justify introducing a systemic dependency that could compromise the network.



## **Funding node operation**

Network coin tax income should provide a base source of compensation for node operators.

This reduces dependence on transaction volume. Operators can receive predictable nominal payments even when network activity temporarily declines.

Compensation still needs to reflect actual contribution. The protocol may consider availability, validation work, storage, reliability and other responsibilities when distributing the available income.

Demurrage income should not simply be distributed to all coin holders in proportion to their balances. That would return the charge to the same participants and weaken the incentive against concentration.

Payments should correspond to work or resources provided to the network.

The amount allocated to node operation should be sufficient to sustain a decentralised set of operators. Excessive rewards transfer unnecessary value towards operators and can increase concentration among large infrastructure providers.

The treasury and protocol should distinguish between the minimum funding required for reliable operation and the additional income available for development.



## **Transaction fees**

Demurrage income can subsidise node operation, but transaction fees should not necessarily be removed immediately.

A transaction fee helps allocate limited network resources and makes large-scale spam expensive. If transactions were completely free, a participant could submit a large volume of unwanted activity unless another effective deterrent existed.

Fees can be reduced towards the minimum required for resource allocation and security. They do not need to generate the entire operating budget.

This separates two functions. The network-coin charge provides predictable infrastructure funding, while transaction fees discourage excessive resource consumption.

If reliable Sybil-resistant identity or another mechanism eventually prevents spam without excluding legitimate users, the network can consider reducing fees further.

The goal is not zero fees as an absolute principle. It is to avoid charging active users more than is necessary.



## **Treatment of staked coins**

Coins committed to consensus provide a productive network function. A staker accepts protocol risks and contributes economic security.

For this reason, staked coins can receive a reduced demurrage rate.

For illustration, idle balances might pay a higher annual rate while properly staked balances pay a lower rate. The exact percentages should be determined through modelling and observed network conditions rather than assumed in advance.

The reduced rate should not eliminate the carrying cost entirely. If staking rewards consistently exceed all holding costs with little risk, large holders may continue increasing their share of the supply.

The relevant measure is the net outcome after staking rewards and demurrage. A mature network seeking dispersion should avoid a structure in which low-risk staking automatically produces continuing concentration.

Node operators can still receive compensation for their work. The objective is to separate payment for operating infrastructure from a passive return available merely because someone owns a large balance.



## **Token exchange liquidity incentive**

Coins supplied as useful token-exchange liquidity should receive a reduced carrying charge.

A programmable network is more valuable when users can exchange its tokens efficiently. Deep liquidity reduces slippage and makes it easier to obtain the network coin required for fees.

The network coin can act as a common pairing across token markets. This reduces the need for every token to maintain a deep direct market against every other token.

The tax reduction should be based on the amount and duration of liquidity supplied. Longer commitments provide more reliable market depth and can justify a greater reduction than temporary deposits.

However, the network must prevent participants from receiving the reduction by creating unused tokens and inactive markets.

Simply depositing coins into a token pairing is not enough to prove productive use. The position should provide meaningful availability to other participants.

Raw exchange volume should not be the sole qualification because users can create artificial transactions between wallets they control.

Eligibility may need to consider several indicators over time, but the criteria should remain simple enough to avoid an unmanageable governance process.



## **Single asset lending incentive**

Coins supplied through a recognised single-asset lending protocol should also receive a reduced demurrage rate.

This gives holders a way to make the coin available without requiring them to take direct exposure to another token.

Borrowers can obtain coins for transaction fees, exchange liquidity and other uses, while lenders receive protocol fees and a reduction in the carrying charge.

Because the deposited and borrowed asset is the same, the protocol avoids the need to compare the network coin with an external asset for ordinary liquidations. This removes one important source of oracle risk.

The arrangement is not free from all risk. Smart-contract failure, inadequate liquidity and governance problems remain possible.

The network should not describe the position as guaranteed or entirely risk-free. Its advantage is that it avoids much of the relative-price risk introduced by multi-asset lending.



## **Balancing liquidity incentives**

The tax reductions for token-exchange liquidity and single-asset lending should be broadly comparable.

If exchange liquidity receives a much larger benefit, users may create artificial tokens and pairings merely to obtain the lower rate.

If lending receives a much larger benefit, too much liquidity may move away from token markets, weakening exchange efficiency.

Comparable rates allow participants to choose according to their actual risk tolerance and intended contribution.

The network can still monitor whether one function is consistently under-supplied. Any adjustment should be gradual and based on sustained conditions rather than temporary market activity.

The objective is to maintain enough coin availability across both functions without creating a complicated schedule of constantly changing incentives.



## **Contract collateral incentive**

Coins used as contract collateral can also receive a reduced carrying charge.

Collateral supports agreements between participants who may not know or fully trust one another. It can provide compensation or create a deterrent if a party breaches the applicable conditions.

The strength of the incentive can depend on the duration for which the collateral is genuinely committed. Coins that can be withdrawn immediately provide less assurance than coins locked for the term of an agreement.

A liquidity position may also serve as contract collateral if the relevant application can verify and control it. This allows one position to provide exchange or lending utility while supporting another agreement.

The network should avoid applying several cumulative reductions to the same coins merely because one position is referenced by several applications. The carrying charge should reflect the underlying economic function of the coins rather than the number of labels attached to them.

Tokens can also serve as collateral. The network coin does not need to dominate every agreement, particularly when stable purchasing power or an application-specific asset is more appropriate.



## **Non-qualifying financial uses**

The network coin can be used in multi-asset lending, stablecoin issuance, collateralised debt positions, derivatives and synthetic assets.

These applications may be useful, but they should not automatically receive a network-level tax reduction.

Many depend on external price information, complex liquidation systems and lists of approved assets. Their failure can place deposited network coins at risk.

They may also lock coins without making them more available for network use. A participant could create a token, deposit network coins as collateral and hold the resulting asset solely to avoid demurrage.

The network should reserve reduced rates for functions with a direct and broadly shared benefit. Other applications can compete for the coin based on their own usefulness and returns.

Nothing prevents participants from using the network coin in these protocols. The distinction concerns whether the network should subsidise that use through a lower charge.



## **A base rate and reduced rates**

The implementation can use a base demurrage rate for ordinary balances and reduced rates for selected positions.

The base rate represents the cost of holding the coin without providing an identified network benefit.

Staking, exchange liquidity, single-asset lending and contract collateral may qualify for lower rates because they support security, availability or agreement enforcement.

The reduced rate should still preserve some carrying cost. Exempting productive positions entirely may allow large holders to preserve and compound ownership through the lowest-risk qualifying activity.

The difference between the base and reduced rates should be sufficient to influence behaviour but not so large that every holder feels compelled to enter a financial protocol they do not understand.

Users should remain free to pay the base rate and retain direct liquidity. The system should not make participation in potentially risky applications a practical requirement for ordinary coin ownership.



## **Selecting the initial rate**

There is no universal demurrage rate suitable for every network and development phase.

A newly launched network may begin without demurrage or with a very low rate while it attracts initial capital and creates productive uses.

Once the network enters the growth phase, a modest charge can provide treasury income and begin redirecting idle balances.

The initial rate should be low enough that users and applications can adapt without a sudden loss of demand. It should be introduced with clear notice and a published schedule.

The network can then evaluate how balances, liquidity, staking, coin price and transaction activity change.

A mature network may support a higher rate if it has strong network effects, useful applications and an effective funding process.

The rate should not be increased merely because the network is capable of collecting more income. Each increase must be justified by the objectives of availability, dispersion, security and productive funding.



## **Relationship with low-risk yields**

The return available from low-risk staking and lending is an important reference point.

If the effective demurrage rate remains below these returns, large holders may preserve or increase their balances through low-risk activity.

Suppose a holder earns 2 per cent from staking and pays an effective demurrage rate of 1 per cent. Their nominal balance can continue growing by approximately 1 per cent before other effects.

If the mature network aims to disperse ownership, the carrying cost may need to exceed the aggregate return available from the lowest-risk strategies.

This does not mean every profitable investment should be eliminated. Participants taking meaningful risk or providing valuable services can still earn positive returns.

The objective is to prevent existing ownership from producing automatic and low-risk concentration.



## **Coin appreciation**

The rate should also be considered relative to expected long-term appreciation.

A holder may lose units while still receiving a substantial increase in purchasing power. During the growth phase, this can help attract investment and fund development.

If appreciation remains far above the carrying charge, passive storage may continue despite demurrage.

The network should not attempt to change the rate in response to every market movement. Short-term prices are volatile and can be influenced by speculation.

Persistent appreciation connected to real network growth may justify a gradual rate increase or a modest expansion of supply.

The relevant concern is the long-term net return from inactivity, not the coin’s price on a particular day.



## **Stable demand**

The demurrage rate must preserve enough demand to support node operation and network security.

A high rate may produce rapid movement into tokens and external assets. Users can then obtain the network coin only at the point of transaction.

This may increase velocity but reduce the coin’s market value and make longer-term compensation less reliable.

The network should monitor whether participants continue choosing the coin for staking, liquidity and collateral when those uses remain optional.

A sustained decline in optional use may indicate that the carrying charge is too high, the incentives are poorly designed or confidence in the network is weakening.

Reducing the rate can restore some demand, but policy changes should remain predictable. Frequent adjustment creates uncertainty and may encourage speculation around governance decisions.



## **Token-money considerations**

The network-coin demurrage rate affects token-based monetary systems operating on the same network.

The native coin has continuing protocol-level demand and is likely to be widely recognised. If it has the same carrying charge as a token while offering greater liquidity and reliability, users may prefer to hold the network coin.

A token intended as a medium of exchange may need a lower rate, more stable purchasing power or other characteristics that make it desirable within its community.

A high network-coin rate gives token communities more room to select their own demurrage policies. A very low native rate can act as a practical ceiling because users can move into the network coin instead of holding a more heavily charged token.

The network should consider this relationship without attempting to control token policies. Token communities remain responsible for determining what their users will accept.



## **Network effects and competition**

Strong network effects can support a meaningful carrying charge.

Users may continue holding and using the coin because the network provides deep liquidity, valuable applications and reliable infrastructure that competitors cannot immediately reproduce.

However, open-source software allows another community to copy much of the technical implementation. A competing network can reduce or remove demurrage and attempt to attract users.

The charge must therefore finance benefits that justify its cost. Reliable node operation, low transaction fees, efficient markets and effective ecosystem funding provide the strongest defence.

The network should not rely solely on user lock-in. Interoperability and automated wallets may make migration easier over time.

A sustainable demurrage rate is one that remains competitive because it improves the network rather than merely extracting value from holders.



## **Treasury expenditure**

The amount collected places a corresponding obligation on the treasury.

The treasury should fund node operation first because the network cannot function without it. Remaining income can support maintenance, security, infrastructure and applications.

As the network matures, some income may support wider public goods if the community has a legitimate process for selecting them.

Funding should be released according to milestones and observable contributions rather than through unnecessary large lump-sum transfers.

Paying contributors directly can reduce the risk that one organisation captures a substantial allocation and uses it to increase governance influence.

The funding process should also avoid repeatedly financing the same participants without evidence of impact.

If the treasury cannot use its income effectively, the network should consider reducing the charge rather than accumulating idle coins.



## **Governance of the rate**

During the genesis and early growth phases, founding entities may help set and adjust the rate because the wider governance process is not yet mature.

This authority should be transparent and limited. Participants need to know who can change the rate, what information they use and how much notice is required.

As the network becomes decentralised, responsibility should move towards the community or a reliable automated mechanism.

Community voting creates legitimacy but can be slow and influenced by large holders. Automation improves consistency but depends on data that may be manipulated.

A combined process may use published indicators to inform infrequent governance decisions without allowing those indicators to change the rate automatically.

Historical data can gradually improve the process. As the network observes the effects of different rates, it can narrow the range of appropriate adjustments.



## **Limits on rate changes**

The protocol should limit how quickly the demurrage rate can change.

A sudden increase could affect collateral, lending and long-term contracts. It could also produce rapid selling as participants attempt to reduce their balances.

A sudden reduction rewards existing large holders and may weaken treasury income before alternative funding is available.

Rate changes should be announced in advance and introduced gradually. Applications should have enough time to update their calculations and users should be able to make informed decisions.

A permitted range can prevent governance from imposing an extreme rate in a single decision. Changing that range should require a more demanding process than making a routine adjustment within it.

Predictability is especially important when the coin is used as a global financial asset.



## **Development-phase adjustments**

The recommended implementation changes gradually across the network’s development phases.

During genesis, the supply remains fixed and demurrage can be absent or minimal. The treasury relies primarily on the initial allocation, while the network develops productive uses and attracts investment.

During growth, a periodic charge begins generating recurring income. Reduced rates encourage staking, exchange liquidity, single-asset lending and collateral.

During maturity, the effective carrying cost can move above low-risk yields so that passive concentration becomes difficult. Treasury governance and protocol maintenance should by then be distributed across the community.

The transition should depend on observable conditions rather than fixed dates. Application use, liquidity, node participation, ownership distribution and treasury capacity provide more useful indicators than the age of the network alone.
